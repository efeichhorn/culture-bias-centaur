\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

All 126 planned adapter fits are complete. Results are descriptive unless an interval is stated. Categorization and lottery uncertainty and calibration checks remain unfinished.

\subsection{Population shifts and predictive fit}

Frozen Centaur fits US responses better in Giving, Taking, and categorization. Categorization NLL is $0.602$ for the US sample and $0.724$ for the Chinese sample. The all-item lottery audit reverses this ordering ($0.549$ versus $0.523$). Thus, the population ordering depends on the task; raw score differences alone do not establish cultural bias.

Fine-tuning population consistently changes unseen-item predictions (Figure~\ref{fig:population}). US-versus-China categorization adapters differ by $26.6$ percentage points in category choice probability (seed range $23.5$--$28.9$). China-versus-US lottery adapters differ by $21.5$ points on tradeoffs ($18.9$--$23.1$), compared with $2.7$ on dominance items. Giving's US, pooled, and Chinese adapters predict $2.39$, $2.99$, and $3.82$ fewer tokens than frozen Centaur; the US-minus-China gap is $1.43$ tokens.

Population matching generally helps within the fine-tuned task. In Giving, pooled fine-tuning beats the other-population adapter by $0.085$ nats for US responses (95\% interval $[0.055,0.114]$) and $0.083$ for Chinese responses ($[0.068,0.096]$). In categorization, own-population adapters beat the other-population adapter in every seed. Own-adapter NLLs are $0.551$ US/$0.700$ China, versus frozen $0.604$/$0.721$ on the same held-out samples. One Chinese seed nevertheless worsens relative to frozen Centaur.

Lottery own-adapter NLLs are $0.448$ US/$0.321$ China, versus frozen $0.541$/$0.517$. Both populations improve in every seed. Own-population matching beats the other-population adapter except in one Chinese seed. These estimates use only eight test people per country per split. Matching the population and improving over the starting model are therefore separate outcomes.

\subsection{Taking transfer does not preserve the human contrast}

Giving-only fine-tuning shifts Taking predictions downward by $2.15$ tokens for US, $2.47$ for pooled, and $3.15$ for Chinese data. The US-minus-China model gap remains positive ($1.00$ token), but the pooled human Taking gap is negative ($-0.88$; 95\% interval $[-1.19,-0.57]$). The human direction also varies across study waves.

The US adapter predicts both Taking samples better. Own-minus-other log-score gains are $0.101$ nats for US responses ($[0.086,0.116]$) and $-0.060$ for Chinese responses ($[-0.071,-0.048]$). A population-dependent shift thus survives task transfer without reproducing the human difference. Its behavioral meaning also changes: fewer tokens imply less generosity in Giving but more generosity in Taking.

\subsection{Benefits beyond response adjustment vary}

Pooled categorization fine-tuning loses to the scalar adjustment by $0.003$ nats on average. Pooled lottery fine-tuning gains $0.025$ nats across all items and $0.016$ on tradeoffs, with positive pooled tradeoff gains in every seed. This benefit is condition-dependent: US-only fine-tuning loses to its adjustment on tradeoffs in all seeds despite gains on dominance items.

Giving-to-Taking transfer gains are $0.071$ nats for US responses ($[0.051,0.091]$) and $0.154$ for Chinese responses ($[0.135,0.171]$). Fine-tuning can therefore improve transfer beyond the tested common response shift while failing to reproduce the population contrast.

These gains do not yet identify richer behavioral learning. Frozen Centaur's mean semantic probability gap between answer orders is $19.4$ points for categorization and $20.2$ for lotteries. Both orders are averaged, but whether fine-tuning gains largely reflect calibration requires the pending order-aware control.
