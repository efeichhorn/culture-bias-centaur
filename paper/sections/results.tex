\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\begin{itemize}
\item All planned fine-tuning fits are complete. The newer dataset results remain descriptive until uncertainty and calibration checks are finished.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{H1: population fit depends on the task}
\begin{itemize}
\item Frozen Centaur fits US responses better in allocation and categorization; Chinese responses slightly better in lottery tradeoffs (Figure~\ref{fig:frozen}).
\item No consistent Western advantage across tasks. Response predictability may contribute to the score differences.
\item Supporting Ruggeri audit: no clear Western advantage. Its study was already represented in Centaur's original fine-tuning data.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{H2: fine-tuning population changes predictions}
\begin{itemize}
\item Clear shifts on unseen items across all three domains (Figures~\ref{fig:population} and~\ref{fig:transfer}).
\item US fine-tuning produces more category choices; Chinese fine-tuning produces more 10-point lottery choices, especially on tradeoff items.
\item Matching the population generally improves within-task fit relative to the other population's adapter. It does not always improve over frozen Centaur.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{H3: shifts transfer, but human differences do not}
\begin{itemize}
\item Giving-only fine-tuning also changes Taking predictions (Figures~\ref{fig:transfer} and~\ref{fig:social}). Population-dependent shifts persist on the untrained task.
\item The model's population gap has the opposite direction to the pooled human Taking gap; the human gap also varies across study waves.
\item The US adapter predicts both Taking populations better. Population matching therefore does not transfer consistently.
\item Fewer tokens mean less generosity in Giving but more generosity in Taking. A similar numerical shift can change behavioral meaning.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{H4: gains beyond response adjustment vary}
\begin{itemize}
\item Categorization: little consistent benefit beyond the simple response adjustment.
\item Taking: mostly positive gains beyond adjustment. Lottery tradeoffs: mixed gains, depending on fine-tuning and evaluation population (Figure~\ref{fig:adjustment}).
\item Answer-order effects are large. Check whether remaining gains reflect calibration before claiming richer behavioral learning.
\end{itemize}
