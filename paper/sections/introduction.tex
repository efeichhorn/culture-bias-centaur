\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\begin{itemize}
\item Centaur predicts behavior across tasks \citep{binz2025centaur}. A good average score can hide that it predicts some populations better than others.
\item Main question: what changes when we change who we fine-tune on?
\item Three things to distinguish: whether predictions change, whether they get better, and whether they capture the differences between human populations.
\item What this study adds: whether fine-tuning on different populations improves predictions, carries human differences over to a new task, and adds more than a general preference for certain answers.
\item Why this matters: if we use Centaur to stand in for people, we need to know whose behavior it captures. Changed predictions alone don't tell us that.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Related work}
\begin{itemize}
\item Centaur: fine-tuning on human behavior and testing on other experiments \citep{binz2025centaur}.
\item Cross-country comparison: \citet{chen2026proxies} compare prompted LLM answers with human economic preferences across 74 countries. The models capture the cultural patterns poorly.
\item Our focus: what happens to predictions, accuracy, and transfer when we change who we fine-tune on. The starting model, prompts, and amount of data stay the same.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hypotheses}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H1:} Frozen Centaur predicts Western samples more accurately.
\item \textbf{H2:} Fine-tuning on different populations changes the predicted behavior.
\item \textbf{H3:} These prediction differences also appear on tasks not used for fine-tuning.
\item \textbf{H4:} Fine-tuning improves predictions more than a simple adjustment of response preferences.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item H2 and H3 are about whether predictions change. Whether they get better is a separate question.
\item H1 mainly compares US and Chinese samples. H3 has one test on a new task: Giving to Taking, both available in the same dataset. The other datasets only test new items within the same task.
\end{itemize}
