\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\begin{itemize}
\item Centaur predicts behavior across tasks \citep{binz2025centaur}. Good average performance may hide differences between populations.
\item Main question: what changes when the people represented in fine-tuning data change?
\item Keep three outcomes separate: a prediction shift, better prediction, and reproduction of human population differences.
\item Contribution: test whether population-dependent adaptation improves prediction and preserves human differences on a new task, and how much it adds beyond a general response preference.
\item Why this matters: using a model as a human proxy requires checking which population patterns it reproduces. A shift after fine-tuning alone does not establish this.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Related work to cover}
\begin{itemize}
\item Centaur: behavioral fine-tuning and generalization across experiments \citep{binz2025centaur}. Connect those tests to the question of population coverage.
\item Cross-country evaluation: \citet{chen2026proxies} compare prompted LLM responses with human economic preferences across 74 countries and find weak recovery of cultural patterns.
\item Our focus: change the population used for fine-tuning while holding the model, prompts, and data budget fixed; compare shifts, accuracy, and transfer. Check the closest adaptation studies before making a novelty claim.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hypotheses}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H1:} Frozen Centaur predicts Western samples more accurately.
\item \textbf{H2:} Population composition during fine-tuning shifts predicted behavior.
\item \textbf{H3:} Population-dependent prediction shifts persist on unseen tasks.
\item \textbf{H4:} Fine-tuning improves predictions beyond a shared response adjustment.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item H2 and H3 concern changes in model behavior; improved accuracy is a separate check.
\item H1 is tested mainly with US and Chinese samples. H3 has one task-transfer test, Giving to Taking; the other datasets test unseen items.
\item Exploratory hypotheses. Country comparisons do not identify culture as the cause.
\end{itemize}
